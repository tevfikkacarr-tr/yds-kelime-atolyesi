@text Over the past few decades, astronomers have catalogued thousands of planets orbiting other stars, known as exoplanets, many of them detected by the transit method. This technique exploits the slight but measurable dimming that occurs when a planet passes in front of its host star as seen from Earth; by monitoring the brightness of thousands of stars simultaneously, space telescopes have been able to identify periodic dips that signal orbiting bodies. The depth of each dip reveals the planet's size relative to its star, while the interval between transits indicates the length of its year. The method has an inherent bias, however: because a transit is visible only when a planet's orbit is aligned almost edge-on to our line of sight, the vast majority of planetary systems remain hidden from it.
